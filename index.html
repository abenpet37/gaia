01 / THE CHALLENGE

BELOW OUR FEET, THE PLANET NEVER STANDS STILL.

Earth's surface is constantly changing. Tectonic plates shift, faults accumulate stress, and landscapes slowly deform. These movements can be difficult to observe directly, especially across vast and remote regions.

Understanding these changes requires consistent observations, reliable scientific data, and tools that turn complex measurements into information people can interpret.

"Every millimeter of movement tells part of a much bigger story."

02 / THE SOLUTION

MEET GAIA. A NEW WAY TO OBSERVE OUR PLANET.

GAIA brings satellite-based Earth observation and geospatial analysis together in one intuitive platform. By examining changes in the Earth's surface, GAIA aims to make ground deformation and tectonic activity easier to explore, understand, and monitor.

03 / THE TECHNOLOGY

FROM SPACEBORNE RADAR TO EARTH-SCALE INSIGHT.

GAIA builds on the potential of the NASA-ISRO NISAR mission, a powerful Earth-observing satellite designed to measure changes across our planet's land and ice surfaces.

NASA-ISRO SYNTHETIC APERTURE RADAR (NISAR) L-BAND & S-BAND
WHY NISAR?

NISAR combines NASA and ISRO expertise to deliver advanced radar observations of our dynamic planet. Its capabilities create new opportunities for studying earthquakes, land movement, ecosystems, and other changes on Earth.

Learn about the NISAR mission — NASA
STEP 01

OBSERVE

RAW RADAR ACQUISITION

NISAR uses advanced synthetic aperture radar to observe Earth's surface, collecting measurements across different conditions and over time.

STEP 02

COMPARE

InSAR PHASE INTERFEROMETRY

Radar observations acquired at different times can be compared using interferometric techniques to investigate changes in surface position.

STEP 03

ANALYZE

GAIA ANALYTICAL LAYER

Geospatial analysis helps transform radar observations into interpretable deformation measurements and displacement information.

STEP 04

VISUALIZE

GAIA INTERFACE

GAIA presents the resulting information through maps, deformation visualizations, and displacement vectors.

Scientific Methodology Note: Raw SAR acquisitions measure radar backscatter and phase; processed InSAR products quantify surface line-of-sight displacement, which GAIA synthesizes into visual exploration layers. Satellite radar monitors ground movement and does not predict exact earthquake timing.

06 / THE BIGGER PICTURE

BETTER OBSERVATIONS. BETTER UNDERSTANDING. MORE INFORMED COMMUNITIES.

Earthquakes and tectonic processes affect communities, infrastructure, and entire landscapes. While earthquakes cannot currently be predicted with precise timing, monitoring the ground helps scientists better understand the processes shaping our planet.

07 / OUR MISSION

MAKING EARTH OBSERVATION MORE ACCESSIBLE.

GAIA was created with a simple belief: understanding our planet should not be limited by the complexity of scientific data.

We are building a platform that connects satellite observations, geospatial analysis, and intuitive visualization to make Earth's changing surface easier to explore.

Our initial focus is the East African Rift Valley, a region of immense geological importance and scientific opportunity.
